NanoTab vs Crossmint: Which Should You Use for AI Agent Payments?

20 November 2026 · 6 min read

Should you use NanoTab or Crossmint for AI agent payments? If you want to charge agents per API call — a paywall in front of your endpoint that collects USDC — use NanoTab. If you want to give users or agents embedded wallets with email/social login, card checkout, and multi-chain support, use Crossmint.

These two solve different halves of the same problem. Crossmint (founded 2022, raised ~$23.6M) is wallet and checkout infrastructure: spin up smart wallets for users or agents with no seed phrases, accept cards and cross-chain crypto, move money across 40+ chains. NanoTab (open-source, 2026) is a lightweight seller-side paywall: wrap any HTTP endpoint so agents pay USDC per call on Base via the x402 protocol, keep 99.9%, done. Here's the honest breakdown.

Direction of money: charging vs enabling

NanoTab answers one question: "how do I get paid when an agent calls my API?" The paywall returns HTTP 402, the caller settles USDC on Base, your endpoint runs. You never touch the buyer's wallet code; you just collect per call.

Crossmint answers a broader one: "how do my users or agents hold money, pay, and move across chains without crypto UX pain?" Embedded wallets with email login, card-to-crypto checkout, minting, and its GOAT SDK for AI agents that need wallets with delegated permissions. It's the infrastructure the paying side often runs on — an agent with a Crossmint wallet is exactly the kind of buyer that pays a NanoTab-protected endpoint.

Fees: NanoTab is cheaper per call; Crossmint charges for breadth

NanoTab self-hosted takes 0.1% per paid call; the managed gateway takes 2% so you run no infrastructure. On a $0.05 API call, that's a fraction of a cent self-hosted.

Crossmint's pricing is plan-based with per-transaction components that vary by product (wallets, checkout, minting) — reasonable for what it does, but you're paying for 40+ chains, fiat ramps, custody options, and support. If your entire business is "$0.05 per agent API call," that breadth is overhead. If your business is "onboard a million non-crypto users," it's the whole point. Compare your actual volume against both pricing pages before deciding.

Onboarding and UX: Crossmint wins clearly

Here's where honesty demands credit to Crossmint. NanoTab is wallet-to-wallet: both sides need a Base wallet with USDC. For crypto-native agents that's frictionless. For a human with a credit card and no wallet, it's a wall.

Crossmint exists to remove that wall — email/social login creates a wallet invisibly, cards buy crypto or pay directly, cross-chain routing hides which network anything settled on. If your buyers are humans, mainstream apps, or agents acting for humans who pay in fiat, Crossmint's checkout is the layer NanoTab deliberately doesn't build.

Chains and scope

NanoTab is USDC-on-Base only, built on the x402 HTTP-402 protocol. One chain, one asset, zero gas for the seller, sub-second settlement. A deliberate constraint that keeps per-call payments dead simple.

Crossmint spans 40+ chains with card and cross-chain support, NFT minting, token creation, and agent wallet tooling (GOAT SDK). Far broader — and necessarily more complex to integrate. Narrow tollbooth vs full payments department.

MCP and agent-framework fit

NanoTab ships an MCP server out of the box — any MCP-compatible agent (Claude, Cursor, custom harnesses) can discover and pay for your endpoint through the standard tool-call flow. Crossmint's GOAT SDK gives agents wallet operations (transfers, swaps, contract calls) across frameworks like LangChain, Eliza, and Vercel AI SDK, with delegated permissions so an agent can transact within limits you set.

Verdict: they're complements, not competitors

Use NanoTab if: you own an API and want agents paying per call in USDC; you want it live today with sub-1% fees; your buyers are crypto-native agents.

Use Crossmint if: you need embedded wallets with email login; you accept cards or multi-chain crypto; your buyers are humans or agents acting for humans paying in fiat.

Use both if: you sell to both audiences — NanoTab's paywall collects per-call USDC from agents, Crossmint's checkout onboards the humans. The tollbooth and the front door, on the same property.

The agent economy needs both ends: endpoints that can charge, and buyers — human or machine — that can pay without friction. Build the side you're missing first, then add the other. (As of November 2026.)
