NanoTab vs Skyfire: Which Should You Use for AI Agent Payments?

20 October 2026 · 6 min read

Should you use NanoTab or Skyfire for AI agent payments? If you're an indie developer or small team that wants agents paying per API call this afternoon with near-zero fees, use NanoTab. If you're an enterprise that needs KYC, procurement controls, and a funded vendor with SLAs, use Skyfire.

Both solve the same core problem — letting AI agents pay for things autonomously — but they come from opposite ends of the spectrum. Skyfire (founded 2023, raised ~$9.5M) built the enterprise-grade "Visa for AI agents" with identity verification, budgets, and compliance tooling. NanoTab (open-source, 2026) is a lightweight x402 paywall: wrap any HTTP endpoint so agents pay USDC per call on Base, keep 99.9%, done. Here's the honest breakdown.

Fees: NanoTab is dramatically cheaper

This is the starkest difference. NanoTab self-hosted takes a 0.1% fee — on a $0.05 API call that's a fraction of a cent. Even the managed gateway (no infrastructure to run) is 2%, and you keep 98%. Skyfire's network fee sits around 2–3% depending on plan, plus potential platform subscription costs for enterprise tiers.

For micropayments — the $0.01–$1.00 per-call range where agentic payments actually live — fee percentage matters enormously at volume. A service doing 100,000 calls a month at $0.05 each ($5,000 revenue) keeps ~$4,995 with NanoTab self-hosted vs ~$4,850–$4,900 with Skyfire. That gap compounds.

Setup complexity: minutes vs days

NanoTab's pitch is one prompt: point it at your HTTP endpoint and it generates the x402 paywall middleware. No account, no signup, no KYB. Your agent gets a wallet, the buyer pays USDC on Base, funds land in your wallet. There is genuinely nothing to sign up for.

Skyfire requires account creation, identity verification for both buyer and seller agents, budget configuration, and API integration against their platform. That's the right tradeoff for a bank or a Fortune 500 — but it's days of procurement-adjacent work before your first paid call. For a weekend project, it's a non-starter.

Compliance and trust: Skyfire wins clearly

Here's where honesty demands credit to Skyfire. If you're a company whose finance team asks "who did we pay and why," NanoTab's answer ("an address on Base") won't satisfy them. Skyfire provides verified agent identities, spend budgets with guardrails, audit trails, and a vendor you can put through procurement. Regulated industries — finance, healthcare, government — effectively require this layer.

NanoTab has an opt-in risk gate (SafeSpend, powered by Jev) that scores transactions, but it's log-only advisory, not enforcement. It won't replace compliance tooling.

Chains and payment rails

NanoTab is USDC-on-Base only, built on the x402 HTTP-402 protocol. That's a deliberate constraint: one chain, one asset, zero gas for the seller, sub-second settlement. If your buyers hold USDC on Base (increasingly the default for agent wallets), it's frictionless. If they don't, it's a hurdle.

Skyfire supports multiple rails including fiat on/off-ramps, which matters for enterprises that settle in dollars and can't hold crypto on a balance sheet. Broader reach, more moving parts.

MCP and agent-framework fit

NanoTab ships an MCP server out of the box — any MCP-compatible agent (Claude, Cursor, custom harnesses) can discover and pay for your endpoint through the standard tool-call flow. Skyfire is API-first with MCP available via adapters; it works, but it's not the native idiom.

Verdict: it depends on who you are

Use NanoTab if: you're an indie dev, researcher, or small team; you want per-call USDC payments live today; you value open-source and self-hosting; fees under 1% matter to you.

Use Skyfire if: you're an enterprise or regulated org; you need KYC, budgets, and audit trails; you want fiat settlement; you need a funded vendor with support SLAs.

Use both if: you sell to both audiences — NanoTab for the long tail of agent buyers, Skyfire for enterprise contracts. They're not mutually exclusive.

The agentic-payments space is early enough that "which platform" matters less than "are you charging agents at all." Pick the one that matches your buyer, ship it, iterate. (As of October 2026.)
