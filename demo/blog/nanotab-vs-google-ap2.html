NanoTab vs Google AP2: Which Should You Use for AI Agent Payments?

23 November 2026 · 6 min read

Should you use NanoTab or Google AP2 for AI agent payments? If you want agents paying per API call today with near-zero fees, use NanoTab — it's live on Base mainnet now. If you're planning an enterprise agent-commerce roadmap where signed payment mandates, card rails, and stablecoin rails all interoperate, follow AP2 — it's the open protocol Google launched in 2026 with 60+ partners including Mastercard, Amex, PayPal, and Coinbase.

These two operate at different layers, and that's the whole point of this comparison. AP2 (Agent Payments Protocol) is an open standard: it defines how agents, users, and merchants exchange cryptographically signed mandates — Intent, Cart, and Payment mandates — so any compliant wallet, merchant, or network can interoperate. NanoTab is a working product: a lightweight x402 paywall that lets any HTTP endpoint charge USDC per call on Base. One is the rulebook; the other is a game you can play today. Here's the honest breakdown.

Protocol vs product: the key distinction

AP2 doesn't move money itself. It standardises the conversation around money: the user signs an Intent mandate ("my agent may spend up to $50 on flights"), the agent and merchant agree a Cart mandate ("this exact basket at this price"), and a Payment mandate authorises the actual transfer on whatever rail both sides support. Disputes get resolved against signed evidence rather than log archaeology. It's genuinely good design — the kind of thing only a platform-scale player with card-network partners could push.

But a protocol doesn't give you a paywall. Today, if you run an API and want agents paying $0.05 per call this afternoon, AP2 gives you a spec to read, not a button to press. NanoTab gives you the button: point it at your endpoint, get x402 middleware, receive USDC on Base. Different jobs.

Fees: NanoTab has a price; AP2 doesn't (yet)

NanoTab self-hosted takes 0.1% — a fraction of a cent on a typical $0.05 call — or 2% through the managed gateway. Simple, published, predictable.

AP2 as a protocol charges nothing; it's open-source under the Linux Foundation-style governance Google set up. Your costs will come from whichever network, wallet, or processor you use to execute AP2 mandates — card interchange if you settle by card, gas and provider fees if you settle in stablecoins. For micropayments in the $0.01–$1.00 range, card-rail settlement through AP2 will likely cost far more per transaction than NanoTab's USDC-on-Base path. For $500 agent-booked purchases, the reverse is true: cards are the only sane rail, and NanoTab can't help you.

Trust and authorisation: AP2 wins clearly

This is where honesty demands credit to AP2. NanoTab's trust model is wallet-to-wallet: the buyer pays, the seller receives, and an opt-in SafeSpend scorer offers advisory risk signals. There's no human-in-the-loop approval chain and no signed proof of what was agreed.

AP2's mandate chain is purpose-built for the "my agent spent $2,000 and I never approved it" problem. Human-signed Intent mandates cap what agents can do; Cart mandates pin exact terms; everything is non-repudiable. If your buyers are consumers or enterprises who need to see — and constrain — what their agents authorise, AP2's model is the right answer, and NanoTab doesn't compete with it.

Settlement rails

NanoTab is deliberately narrow: USDC on Base, via the x402 HTTP-402 flow. One chain, one asset, sub-second settlement, zero gas for the seller. If your buyers hold USDC on Base — increasingly the default for agent wallets — it's frictionless. If they pay by card, it's a non-starter.

AP2 is deliberately broad: the core spec is payment-method agnostic, with extensions for cards, stablecoins, and bank transfers. Partners like Coinbase are building x402-flavoured stablecoin execution under AP2 mandates, while card networks handle the fiat side. Breadth is the selling point — and the implementation burden.

MCP and agent-framework fit

NanoTab ships an MCP server out of the box: any MCP-compatible agent discovers and pays for your endpoint through the standard tool-call flow. AP2 is designed to sit alongside MCP and Google's A2A protocol — discovery and communication handled there, payment authorisation handled by AP2 mandates. They're complementary layers, not competitors, and in principle a future NanoTab could accept AP2-signed mandates as its authorisation mechanism.

Verdict: different layers, different timelines

Use NanoTab if: you're an indie dev, researcher, or small team; you want per-call USDC payments live today; you value open-source and self-hosting; your buyers are agents holding crypto.

Follow AP2 if: you're an enterprise or platform; you need human-approved spending mandates with audit-grade evidence; you must support cards alongside stablecoins; you're planning 2027 agent-commerce infrastructure.

Use both if: you monetise APIs for agents now with NanoTab while designing your AP2-compliant checkout for card-paying customers later. The per-call tollbooth and the mandate-based checkout serve different transactions.

The agentic-payments space is early enough that "which approach" matters less than "are you charging agents at all." Ship the paywall today; adopt the standard as it matures. (As of November 2026.)
